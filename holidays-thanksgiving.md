
# The summarized story of American Thanksgiving

Thanksgiving in the USA, unlike others like [Christmas](holidays-christmas.md), is a mostly fake holiday built around a dubious situation.

## The first, not-directly-related history

At one time, there were some English colonists in 1620 seeking passage to the New World (the continent of North America), and they were composed of two very different groups:

1. Pilgrims, who were [Puritan Protestants fleeing persecution](history-church-9_renaissance.md) called Brownists
2. Strangers, who were non-Puritans and there for [business reasons](entrepreneur-1_what.md)

120 passengers left, 90 on the Mayflower and 30 on the Speedwell, but the Speedwell had to return because of a few leaks in the ship. There is some speculation that the crew of the Speedwell intentionally sabotaged the ship to avoid making the dangerous trans-Atlantic voyage. Some passengers abandoned the trip, but 12 others were added to the Mayflower.

The Mayflower left Plymouth, England on 1620-09-06 with 102 passengers. During the trip, the second month was particularly turbulent, and one passenger died.

They arrived on 1620-11-09 off the coast of Cape Cod. They wanted to travel southward to the designated landing site, but they were dangerously low on resources and winter was coming.

They had to survive the first winter on board the ship because they couldn't build settlements, and 45 of the 102 died from the cramped conditions and limited living space. The Puritans may have had a celebration that emulated the Feast of Tabernacles (Leviticus 23:39), but it wasn't called Thanksgiving and was largely designed to honor the dead.

In March that next year, they met the local native tribe. There was general distrust, but they exchanged gifts and established a formal peace treaty. Further, over the next year or so the natives taught them quite a few skills to survive, especially a particular native named Squanto. The Pilgrims held a feast with them, probably sometime around October, where both sides contributed. The actual encounter involved 90 armed natives, who heard the celebratory gunfire, and they may have arrived either because they presumed the colonists were under attack or because they thought the colonists had betrayed the treaty.

This would be the end of the actual story in late 1621, but it has some [political connections](politics-conservativeliberal.md) as well and has been expanded.

## The expanded not-directly-related history

Essentially, the Mayflower Compact was an early type of [leftist](politics-leftism.md) agreement, somewhat inspired by a misattribution of Acts 2:42-47 and Acts 4:32-5:11 in the Bible, and largely by Thomas More's and Plato's books both named Utopia. It basically collected everyone's proceeds, then gave every person a single share of that collective property.

The problem is that in a system where everyone receives the same portion, [nobody will want to work](https://gainedin.site/capitalism/). Between that and a few years of unusually awful weather, they were doing very poorly by early 1623. At that point, the colony abandoned their collective system and people were free to keep everything they had (essentially using 2 Thessalonians 3:10 as a counteraction to their original ideas).

However, summer of that year was still unusually hot with no rain. One of the leaders William Bradford called the colony to a day of repentance and prayer. On that day, they saw rain and gave glory to God, and the rain continued favorably into the fall.

For that reason, they gave a second thanks-giving day feast.

There's also broader history that involves the eradication of the native population over the next decades through plagues and many land disputes, though the original Pilgrims don't seem to have been involved in the circumstances.

However, this didn't become an indefinite tradition until over 200 years later in the 1860s.

## Making a holiday

There really weren't many clear celebrations, though there were various celebrations in different regions between the 1770s to the 1800s for honoring the dead and giving thanks to God.

During the early years of the United States, there was support for a Thanksgiving DAy by George Washington and John Adams, but Thomas Jefferson didn't want to recognize it as a formal holiday in 1808 because he believed it was a state-sponsored religious observance. This was a very heated topic, and Jefferson didn't explain his reasoning to the public. However, he was portrayed as an atheist by his Federalist opponents.

Ironically, Jefferson had had his own Thanksgiving and Prayer day in 1779 when he was governor. He saw that the states had the free license to declare a religious holiday, and practiced it himself, but saw that the federal government shouldn't have that power.

James Madison in 1815 did reinstate a Thanksgiving Proclamation, but there wasn't really a holiday attached to it in any formal capacity.

Much later, in 1863, an elderly activist named Sarah Josepha Hale sent a petition to Abraham Lincoln to make Thanksgiving Day a fixed national festival. This was in the middle of a very awful civil war, and Lincoln liked the idea, so he ordered all government offices in Washington to close the last Thursday of November. The entire history of the Pilgrims was woven into the celebration at this time.

The entire tradition holds itself together with a specific meal:

- Tyically a turkey, though many people use ham instead if they're awful at or too lazy to [cook](cooking.md)
  - Economically, the standard 12-20-pound turkey is absurdly excessive, and *way* too much for any gathering below 10 people.
- Stuffing, which is often baked outside the turkey
- Mashed potatoes and gravy
- Dinner rolls
- Cranberry sauce of some sort, often from a can
- Other sides like green bean casserole, mac and cheese, and salad
- Pie at the end, usually pumpkin, but often pecan or apple
  - Technically, "pumpkin" is actually winter squash, but most people don't know because it comes out of a can.

## The capitalist holiday spinoffs

Later, in 1939, store owners pressured Franklin D. Roosevelt to move Thanksgiving Day to the third Thursday in November to create mroe shopping days between Thanksgiving Day and Christmas. FDR moved it, but people kept celebrating the same, so he made it the fourth Thursday of November.

Shortly after Thanksgiving, Philadelphia held an Army-Navy football game that Saturday. In the 1950s, Philadelphia police used the term "black Friday" to describe the chaos from shoppers and tourists who flooded the city at that time. While [advertisers](marketing.md) tried to shift the term to "Big Friday" to remove the negative connotation, it never stuck. Eventually, however, they [spun the story](people-image-distortion.md) to reflect the [accounting](money-accounting.md) concept of "in the black" (meaning profitable).

Black Friday, like other relatively unimportant [marketing](marketing.md) days like [Memorial Day](holidays-omnibus.md) and [Valentine's Day](holidays-valentines.md), expanded due to the promise of low prices. At its peak in the early 2010s, people would wait outside retail stores overnight just to get an affordable TV. Eventually, retailers started supplying *very* few of those sale items (thereby provoking people to enter the store and potentially buy something else), and most Black Friday sales as of the 2020s are often *worse* than normal prices.

Further, to expand the sales, there have been other marketing holidays and [counter-trends](people-trends.md):

- Cyber Monday gives online sales for people to buy things [over the internet](cs-browser.md).
- Small Business Saturday tries to provoke people to buy from small businesses.
- Buy Nothing Day is an anti-consumerist day of abstinence.

## Non-American Thanksgiving

Since the tradition of Thanksgiving is simply about giving thanks, it's based on a broader "harvest festival" theme and honoring the dead that has been around for thousands of years. For that reason, Canada and the UK have their own Thanksgiving with some similar patterns, and most societies across the planet celebrate some form of it by another name.
