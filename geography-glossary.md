
# Glossary of geography terms

Archipelago - a large group of islands

Atoll - an archipelago or chain of islets connected by a coral reef that nearly encloses a lagoon

Basin - an area of land that drains into a body of water

Bay - a recessed, coastal body of water connecting to a larger body of water

Beach - a coastal area that has a clear shoreline

Butte - a hill with a flat top

Cape - a point or head of land protruding into a body of water

Cave - a carved-out portion of a larger mass of rock

Channel - aka strait, a narrow body of water that joins two larger bodies of water

Chaparral - a dry region of mostly thickets

Cliff - a mass of land with a vertical drop

Coast - aka shore, a portion of land that descends gradually into water, often has pebbles or sand

Delta - a triangle-shaped area where a river meets a body of water and deposits sediments

Desert - a region of land with less than 10 inches of rainfall a year

Dune - a pile of sand in a desert

Fjord - a channel with steep slopes on both sides

Forest - a large collection of trees

Geyser - a natural hot spring that intermittently ejects water and steam from underground

Glacier - a large mass of ice that slowly flows over a landmass

Gulf - a large area of sea or ocean partially enclosed by land

Hill - a well-defined natural elevated mass of land that's smaller than a mountain

Hot spring - a natural spring that produces warm water

Iceberg - a large mass of ice floating in the ocean

Island - a section of land surrounded by a body of water

Islet - a small island

Isthmus - a narrow strip of land connecting two larger masses of land

Jungle - a forest with dense vegetation, often interchanges with rainforest

Lagoon - a shallow body of water

Lake - an enclosed body of water, can be fed into with rivers

Marsh - a swamp that has more grasses than wooded plants

Mesa - a plateau with cliffs around it

Mountain - a large, well-defined natural elevated mass of land

Oasis - a fertile or green area within a desert where the water table approaches the surface

Ocean - a vast body of water

Peninsula - a section of land extending into a body of water

Plain - a large area of flat land with few trees

Plateau - aka tableland, an elevated plain

Prairie - a plain with grasslands

Rainforest - a forest with extremely high precipitation (over 160 inches annually), meaning far more biomass throughout it, often interchanges with jungle

River - a large flowing body of water

Sea - a vast body of water that's smaller than an ocean

Soil - dirt

Sound - a smaller body of water connecting to a sea or ocean

Spring - a small, natural source of water that flows from the ground

Stream - a small flowing body of water

Subsoil - dirt beneath the topsoil

Swamp - a valley that is frequently flooded

Thicket - aka copse, a dense growth of underbrush

Topsoil - the upper layer of soil, usually 5-10 inches deep

Tundra - a plain where the subsoil is always frozen

Valley - a lower-altitude relatively flat region of land

Waterfall - a river or stream that travels off a cliff
