
# Speculative computers

The realm of speculative computers runs parallel to all other speculative science fiction.

However, the ideas, like Jules Verne's 19th-century depictions of our time, may have some merit, though it's a moonshot over how accurate any of it really is.

## Advanced integration: healthcare

Giving additional power to monitor [a person's health](body-health.md) is a generally good thing, and we see a *ton* of advancements by Big Medi-Pharma in that department.

Since integrated circuits have become so small, this is no longer a realm of science fiction.

The largest issue, however, is that placing any hardware *inside* the body need to either:

1. Maintain its composition indefinitely, or at least stay safe to the patient as it decomposes.
2. Regenerate to last indefinitely.

We haven't been able to resolve that hurdle.

## Advanced integration: body enhancement

The development of organs and cybernetic enhancements is technically *possible*, but inferior to having natural organs and limbs.

This will develop as a very slow [trend](people-trends.md), divided as general classes according to the organ's utility:

1. People who would otherwise be permanently disabled will consent to experimental treatment.
2. With enough research, those people will significantly outperform able-bodied people.
3. Eventually, able-bodied athletes will experiment with the benefits of replacing that specific part.
4. There will be plenty of [political conflict](politics-conservativeliberal.md) over the modifications, similar to steroid use in the 20th century.
5. Eventually, there will be a place for that sort of competition (e.g., "The Augmented Leagues").
6. As the athletes succeed, children will see them as role models and will readily accept modifications.
7. As the children reach [maturity](maturity.md), society will treat it as a standard part of life.

## Advanced integration: brain

Brain-computer integrations are quite popular to imagine, likely because they're the most readily convenient to imagine their improved utility, but also because they're likely to happen within our lifetimes.

Sadly, all aspects of [Big Tech violating our privacy](cs-bigtech.md) are 100x worse. Instead of tracking the [decisions](mind-decisions.md) people make, the ability to read and send signals into a brain effectively gives them control *over* decisions directly.

The [trend](people-trends.md) will likely advance later than other biological enhancements, mostly due to the [privacy](safety-security-privacy.md) risks everyone is subconsciously aware of.

## Financial control

While some technologies like [blockchain](cs-blockchain.md) represent the backbone of new currencies, there is a speculation that a computer could theoretically control or predict markets.

This will likely never work, since there are simply too many complexities to centrally control [an economy](money-economics.md).

However, it will persist as a speculation forever because of [greed](morality-evil.md).

## Interstellar travel

Computers already assist with space travel, but there are presuppositions that we could find ways to bend spacetime to travel farther and faster.

Much of this faces hard constraints from scale. Even while [quantum physics](science-physics-quantum.md) can move an atom or two via superstate manipulation, the complexities exponentiate with every additional atom. Even wrangling absurdly high-powered computers is nearly impossible.

## Quantum computing

The backbone of most speculative science fiction is built on [quantum computing](cs-speculative-quantum.md) these days. It's mostly a pipe dream and an opportunity for [fundraising](mgmt-7_changes.md).

## Highly speculative

There are quite a few dramatic computing systems created purely from science fiction and unlikely to ever happen. Or, at least, unlikely to happen for thousands of years (assuming most [religions](religion.md) are incorrect about their [apocalypse](theology-end.md)).

Computronium is a hypothetical material that is programmable all the way to its atomic level.

- It has also been referenced as a state: the ideal form of computing by using a specific amount of matter.

A matrioshka brain is the idea of using multiple layers of Dyson spheres (energy generators that fully encompass stars) to make nesting Dyson spheres.

- The idea is that each Dyson sphere would draw energy and give off waste heat, which the next Dyson sphere would use as computational inputs, with its result being waste heat for another sphere.

Connected but not directly related to computing is the Kardashev scale, which measures a civilization's technological advancement by how much they can control all energy in a physical system:

- Type I can access all available energy from a planet and store it for consumption.
- Type II can access all energy from a star, such as from a Dyson sphere.
- Type III can access all energy from a galaxy, including black holes and matter or energy we're not aware of.
- There are other proposed types that take this farther (e.g., universe, multiverse).
