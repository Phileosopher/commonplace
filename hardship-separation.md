
# How to handle separation from loved ones

There are many reasons why a separation from a loved family member can happen.

- [Wars](people-conflicts-war.md)
- [Narcissistic abuse](hardship-abuse-narcissist.md)
- [Cult membership](people-culture-cults.md)

## Don't blame yourself

Unless you can pinpoint what you did wrong, don't take their separation personally.

- Many times, people will choose to estrange themselves out of [fear](mind-feelings-fear.md) over what may happen otherwise.
- Other times, people can be [manipulated](people-image-distortion.md) into believing they're making the right decision.

## Do whatever you can to stay in touch

Even if it's mailing a letter, try to send information to them about how much you love them.

- They may not always receive it, but you will never know your impact until much later.

## Trust God

Get your situation sorted out with [God](religion-answers.md).

- *You* don't have connection with them, but God certainly does.

## When you are a victim of parental alienation

Since you're facing a type of injustice from your [significant other](relationships-marriage.md), you're facing multiple issues:

1. The injustice from their actions against you.
2. The injustice from their actions against your child.
3. Social effects over when others express very little sympathy.
4. System-wide issues that come from courts and other authority structures.

To avoid being overwhelmed, try to take each allegation separately.

- You'll likely lose [friends](people-friends.md) alongside the alienation, and that's [likely a good thing](people-friends-bad.md).
- The unjust actions against your children will come later, so focus on [recovering](hardship.md) from your [betrayal](hardship-betrayal.md) first and foremost.

Many times, courts can inadvertently magnify a parental alienation situation by not acting expediently.

- As much as possible, occupy your time and energy with things you *can* control.
- The system *will* move slower than you'd prefer, so expect and learn patience.

Part of the lack of sympathy comes through how parental alienation abusers tend to state similar-sounding allegations from the outside to the victims.

- If a person were to hear both sides of a story, they'd see that both people are alleging mostly the same thing (e.g., they abused them, psychological abuse, etc.).
- Further, the victim, due to [how the final stage of narcissism affects people](hardship-abuse-narcissist.md), tends to be more silent, which means ~60-70% of parental alienation on social media is by the alienators.

### Stay mindful of their hardship

A child's alienation will be harder on them than on the alienated parent.

- While a father or mother losing a child is awful, the child receives a more severe trauma.

Most of what a child does in a separation is a pure survival instinct.

- They are forced into a situation where they must quietly accept their parent's version of the story to avoid adverse repercussions.
- When they're younger, this can often mean their entire [value system](values.md) will have to adapt to survive.

Childrens' memories are more malleable and easily influenced by emotions, suggestions, and repeated messages.

- Therefore, constant negative statements about a parent can easily register as true memories, even if they are inaccurate or exaggerated.
- Very often, children will carry this confusion far into adulthood, which represents [trust](mind-trust.md) issues with the alienated parent, trust issues with people, [relationship](relationships-dating.md) issues, and emotional pain due to distant or lost memories.

Often, children will find cracks in their estranging parent's narrative once they reach adolescence.

- They will frequently reassess the thoughts they were given, and will often develop a different view than they had before.
- Many times, they'll secretly reach out to the other parent or seek answers.

### Your conversations with them

When they've been conditioned to say things, their phrases and behaviors have similar patterns.

- If they once had a warm, open relationship with a parent they'll often rapidly become distant, cold or even hostile to a parent to them.
- They use adult language instead of childlike expressions.
- They speak with black-and-white thinking, with no room for nuance.
- Their messages are repeated consistently, with little variance in their approach or tone.
- They'll appear to feel guilty around the estranged parent's affection.
- They'll make distance with extended family like grandparents or siblings.

Their statements are signals of [abuse](hardship-abuse-narcissist.md), and aren't conclusions they came to themselves.

- In some ways, recovering from parental alienation is [cult deprogramming](people-culture-cults.md).

The next time you see your children, expect them to state things they were conditioned to say.

- "I don't want to see you anymore" / "Stop contacting me"
  - This sounds like they're [setting boundaries](people-boundaries.md), but they're often reflecting intense pressure from other caregivers.
- "You've never been there for me" / "You're the reason why everything went wrong"
  - It appears to be a grievance or blame, but they're often repeating the phrase they heard from the alienating parent.
- "Mom/dad says you're dangerous"
  - While it may seem like they're concerned, they're simply relaying the internalized warnings they received.
- "I just feel uncomfortable around you"
  - They often do feel discomfort, but it comes from having to reconcile the new information you present compared to what they've been told.
- "I don't remember anything good"
  - The other parent's negative memories have often suppressed memories they once had.
- "I'm choosing this myself"
  - They've often been coached to state autonomous-sounding statements, but they're often acting under the influence of the alienating parent.
- "They would never lie to me"
  - This shows their dependency on one side of the story, but it often isn't simply out of [trust](mind-trust.md).
- "If you loved me, you'd leave me alone"
  - It appears to be a boundary, but is usually emotional conditioning tied to their rejection.
