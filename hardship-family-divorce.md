
# How to handle divorce

Divorce is the destruction of the most basic institution of human connection.

- It's impossible to *not* be heavily affected by a divorce, even if you're the one filing for it.

## How to look at your divorce

Your life with that person is over, and you must accept that:

- Many times, the shock of the divorce comes through the factual reality that confronts any denial about the marriage ending.
- Even if your ex-spouse wants you back, after a few years they won't be that person you knew anymore.
- Very likely, the reason they left was *because* they're not the person you knew.

The emotional stages of a divorce are relatively common, though the context may vary on the timing.

- Everyone will go through [the stages of a great loss](mind-feelings-sadness.md), and it usually takes about 12-14 months, but possibly longer if the divorce lasted more than a decade.
  - It's perfectly typical and healthy to feel [shame or guilt](mind-feelings-shame.md) about the divorce.
  - It's common to have vacillating feelings, but also shifting moods week-to-week (e.g., despair, anger, numbness, sadness).
  - It's also common to have extremely low energy while grieving, and it's worth getting back into a [routine](habits.md) to normalize things again.
- [Physical pain from the grieving](mind-feelings-happiness-stress.md) is also common.
- The timing is key on whether we "should" be grieving.
  - Within the first few weeks or months, we are guaranteed to experience shock and grief over it.
- However, at the same time, there are many emotional changes that will happen as well to create [more hardship](hardship.md):
  - Whatever financial assets you shared with your spouse (i.e., most of them) will be placed in legal limbo.
  - Your ability to see your children will shift around (usually less if you have majority custody, or more if you don't).
  - You may be required to [move](home-moving.md) across state lines or pick up a [new career](jobs-1_why.md).

Often, a divorce is pre-planned by one partner for years, while the other partner is utterly oblivious to that betrayal.

- Frequently, lesser betrayals arise before that point:
  - Sexual infidelity
  - Unreliability as a provider or supporter
  - Physical assault
  - Emotional distance
  - A desire for "something better"

Legally, a divorce is the termination of a civil agreement.

- Beyond criminal matters (e.g., kidnapping, assault), the courts will see everything as a mere civil dispute.
- The case can be resolved out of court when both parties are willing to work with each other, but a disputed case becomes a nightmare on several fronts:
  1. The battle over everyone's possessions, if any.
  2. Alimony payments, if applicable.
  3. Custody and visitation rights, if any children are involved.
  4. Child support, if custody and visitation rights are involved.

While it's not common, at its most severe a one-sided at-fault uncontested divorce can do the following:

1. Alimony payments for life (especially if the marriage was over 10 years) at an imputed income (basically the expected income even if someone isn't working)
2. Child support for each child until they're adults
3. No custody of the child and extremely limited, supervised visitations as little as once a month
4. A majority stake given to them of your financial estate, especially with allegations of abuse

## Your divorce will ripple across others

Every relationship with your family and friends will change:

- [Your children](parenting-children.md) will endure the hardship of losing their parents for the rest of their lives.
- Your family and friends will often be devastated by the news, and some will judge you negatively for it.

## Try to avoid mixing your children into it

If you have children in the situation, you *must* avoid mixing them further into it.

- The older a child experiences parental separation, the harder they'll take it.

Contrary to popular opinion, divorce itself is *not* the determining factor for how well children handle life later.

- The key detail is how the children observe their parents [dealing with conflict](people-conflicts.md).
- The scope of divorce simply happens to be with people who are fundamentally [awful at handling conflicts](people-conflicts-bad.md).

The situation is between you and your spouse, so explicitly and constantly clarify a few things:

1. It's not their fault in any way.
2. They can't do anything to fix it.
3. You (and their other parent, if applicable) still love them.

They'll likely cause more trouble in their reactions to the change, so scale back your discipline with them.

- Your divorce has at least partially eroded any authority you once had with them.
- They're very anxious and angry because you've destroyed the foundation for their entire existence.
- If they developmentally regress or lash out, they're often trying to recreate the "old" situation to get their other parent back.
- They must figure out their own healthy perspective of the world, but their minds aren't equipped to do it.

Most of their innocence will disappear by the time the divorce is settled, so be *completely* honest with them about *everything*.

- They can handle the truth, but [lies](people-lying.md) will destroy your relationship with them later.
- Even if you want to protect them or yourself, they will likely find out because their [feelings](mind-feelings.md) are more affected by it than you.

When changing custody with the other parent, symbolically associate the timing with a portion of the routine (such as after lunch), and *not* what the clock says.

- If interaction with your ex is particularly hostile, do it in a public place or outside a police station.

The more you can add new lifestyle elements, the easier they'll transition into a new way of life.

- Having a "substitute" father or mother (i.e., other family) can allay some of their anxieties, though it'll never be perfect.

In the event of an extremely [evil](morality-evil.md) action (such as [parental alienation](hardship-separation.md)), the safest thing you can do is to prevent that parent from having more means to abuse the relationships.

- Without [criminal](legal-crimes.md) evidence, you likely won't gain full custody of the child, especially if you are a male.
  - Further, expect to pay child support for the privilege to even interact with your child, since child support triggers the court orders for visitation.
- A coparenting arrangement after a high-conflict divorce is a dangerous arrangement because it forces you to be in the presence of a toxic individual.
  - Often, that parent will provoke you, then document your reaction to the provocation.
  - They can also agree to things, then deny or reframe what you both said.
  - This then becomes a question of "he said, she said", with nobody being able to know for sure what was actually said.
- However, you can definitely request the court place an order of protection that forbids you and the other parent from adversely contacting each other, which includes disparaging remarks about the other parent.

If there was a ton of fighting before the divorce, some kids will actually see a divorce as a *relief*.

Learn gratitude for the time you *do* get with your children, especially since many custody arrangements can lead to [parental alienation](hardship-separation.md).

## Going to court

The divorce process is a drawn-out process that can take months to years, but it can go longer.

- Fault divorces often have a shorter statute (e.g., 1 year), but no-fault divorces can go for 2-4 years.
- Part of the [trauma](hardship-ptsd.md) of divorce comes in how our desire for [justice](morality-justice.md) is rarely satisfied in a timely manner, as well as the limits of judges to observe the details of a case.

Divorces run through a relatively straightforward, but long, process.

- Often, the defendant/respondent will have a notice to appear.
  - They can submit a motion to dismiss or motion to show cause, depending on the context.
- There may be a mediation, who is a third party between the spouses.
  - Sometimes it'll be a mandatory mediation and will go absolutely nowhere.
  - Alternately, it may be a collaborative divorce with lawyers on each side without going to court.
- However, most divorces have traditionally involved a judge deciding the marital assets and custody arrangement.
- The petition is initially delivered, with the recipient party having to file a response (or answer) often within 20-30 days. Without the response, the divorce can be a default divorce where the petitioner gets what they requested.
- After divorce is initiated, the discovery phase begins where each party states what they want to say about issues, the debts and assets owed by each party, and witnesses who may be asked to testify.
- The court may create temporary orders that bind the spouses about physical use of the family home, child custody, physical protection of a spouse or child, bans on marital property use or transfer, taking unnecessary debt, and changes to [insurance](money-insurance.md).
- The divorce then goes to a trial, but can have a settlement reached if both parties can consent to an arrangement.
- The finalized divorce will then be laid out in a legal document (or final judgment of divorce, or divorce decree).
- Either party can appeal, but there are other conditions that apply, and both parties have to get new lawyers, and not all appeals are heard by the judges.

Ideally, you'll be able to settle an agreement with them.

- However, most marriages break apart because at least one of the partner's expectations or desires has become utterly irrational.
- Many times a settlement is possible, but it *will* be a compromise that both of you won't completely like.
  - Don't waste time or legal fees on any type of settlement if they're completely unwilling to shift on their preferences.

Even if you have religious objections to divorce, a long-term absence usually merits a legal separation.

- By filing for legal separation, you get all the legal benefits of a divorce, but without a formalized divorce.
- Further, if you or your ex seek divorce later, it becomes easier to do because everything was already sorted through during the legal separation.

You can definite go pro se (self-represent), though judges and attorneys will want you to believe it's impossible.

- The judge will not go easily on you, but you can definitely do it yourself.
  - The judge is a public servant, and you can definitely be assertive, even though you should *never* show anger toward them.
- Firstly, make sure you document ongoing and continued efforts by your ex to prevent access to your children.
  - This should reflect as far back as you can remember.
  - Avoid using the word "parental alienation", and instead use "gatekeeping" and "custodial interference".
- Firstly, avoid direct contact with your ex without records.
  - Never, *ever* have a private phone conversation with your ex unless you've recorded the interaction or have a witness present.
  - Take screenshots of text message conversations, then reflect those conversations that indicate their acceptance and then rejection of specified parenting time.
- Second, do research for your case.
  1. Call up the courthouse with your case number, then find the contact info for the court clerk for the judge working with your case.
  2. Give a friendly introduction via email to the court clerk, then ask for the names of previous hearings that were recently litigated with the judge regarding the enforcement of what you plan to file.
     - Ideally, you need a few examples each of where the judge ruled in favor of the motion and against it.
  3. Take the names, case numbers, and hearing dates to the court records department and submit a request for an audio transcript (FTR) of the hearings (they're about $10 and don't take long to retrieve).
     - This will give you a good indicator of what the judge expects.
  4. Learn the rules for submitting evidence in your state, and pay attention to how evidence is submitted while reviewing the FTR.
  5. If you have free time, visit the judge's courtroom and check the docket to see if you can sit in on a hearing similar to your case.
  6. Stop at the family law help desk to learn any other litigation you need to know.
  7. Even while self-representing, it's worth getting an attorney's input to know how to properly present your case to the judge.
     - Call the local state bar and ask if they have a lawyer referral service for low-income people.
  8. Beyond an attorney, use Google Scholar and other online resources like YouTube to find precedent and applicable case law.
- Make sure you have a clear, easy-to-understand case by writing it out articulately.
  - You're not seeking to explain why your ex is doing anything or what their motive is, but instead showing a clear pattern of court-ordered violations with clear facts.
- If you need help filling out your documents, look for a legal clinic in your area.
  - Prepare a hearing memorandum and submit it 1-2 days before the scheduled hearing, which will also require the opposing attorney to also receive a copy by email.
- Carry two extra copies of all your exhibits in separate binders: one for the court and one for your ex.
  - You don't need to submit them until you ask the court to review it.
  - If you ave anyone else who can vouch for the fact that you haven't received parenting time, make sure they're there as well.
- [Practice your testimony as much as you can](education-how.md): to your friends, family, and yourself in the mirror.
  - If you've filed the motion, you will typically receive the opportunity to speak first, and should give the reason for your motion and how you intend to prove it with evidence or witness testimony.
- When your ex or witnesses are speaking on record, stay aware of what to listen for or when to raise objections:
  - Hearsay is "he said, she said"
  - Relevance is any off-topic testimony or evidence
  - Non-responsive is the witness not answering the question
  - Speculation is anything asked or stated that was purely speculative
  - Leading the witness is provoking questions to give specific testimony
  - Argumentative is when testimony or questioning is combative in any way
- When giving your testimony, give eye contact with the *judge*, not the lawyer, when answering questions.
- The purpose is to ultimately force the judge to follow state statutes and case law on public record.
  - Self-representation prevents the judge and opposing attorney from meeting in chambers beforehand.
  - You always have the right to have every word you say in the court about your case to be on public record.
  - Any misconduct by the judge or opposing attorney during the hearing will open them up for possible scrutiny by their state bar.

## High-conflict divorces and children

Most of the time, well-meaning partners will walk right into a high-conflict divorce without awareness of the other person's bad faith.

- They will choose to believe the other person was simply traumatized, hurt, or working through issues.
  - Up until the end, they'll believe the best of their partner because they've wished for it to be the case.
- In reality, their choice was poorly made, but is highly common.
  - Most people have a hard time believing the absolute moral emptiness of some people, especially if they choose to [marry them](relationships-marriage.md) because it reflects poorly on their own ability to make decisions.

Men, more than women, are extremely vulnerable during divorces.

- The legal precedent of the past has arranged a context where women have more [power](power.md) than men, especially with respect to custody.
  - Men are about 15x more likely to be [incarcerated](hardship-incarceration.md) and have an order of protection placed against them.
- Many [Cluster B](mind-neurodivergence-clusterb.md) women will use the "silver bullet strategy", which will involve many false allegations in the hopes that something will stick.
  - These allegations create mental conditions similar to being a prisoner of war, and men are 9 times more likely to commit suicide in these contexts due to [depression](hardship-depression.md), [betrayal PTSD](hardship-ptsd.md), and [sleep disorders](sleep-insomnia.md).
- Society also doesn't know how to handle the context.
  - Most people without experience with [narcissistic abuse](hardship-abuse-narcissist-stages.md) will blame men.
  - Most institutions are poorly prepared for the trauma men face, and often will either ignore them or misdiagnose the problem.
  - Many women are trained to believe that all women must be believed about all allegations.
- The sad reality is that men would be happy to pay to gain access to their children.
  - The women don't even trust that the men would pay in a good faith agreement for child support, so it has to get thrown in front of a court.
- The only way this can be prevented in our modern highly litigious and [morally bankrupt](morality.md) society is to get a prenuptial or midnuptial agreement, at least with respect to custody.

Sometimes, you may have an order of protection placed against you.

- No contact orders and domestic violence restraining orders are given out by the police with very flimsy or nonexistent evidence.
  - The general approach is to err on the side of caution by preventing anyone from killing anyone else.
- However, a victim of a falsely placed order of protection can often be devastated.
  - They can't go home, meaning they'll be cut off from their possessions and often their car.
  - When it applies to their children, they won't be able to see their children without a ton of classes on anger management, battering, parenting, and coparenting.
  - They will be forced to pay for visitation simply to be with or play with their children.
- Often, the order of protection is the springboard for custody disputes in the courts.
  - The parent will allege that the other parent didn't want to see the children, even though the parent is estranging them.

Judges aren't stupid, and your situation is usually something they've seen before.

- Family court is simply looking at how you'll behave under pressure, not whether you love your child.
  - Most judges want to see that a parent is safe, reliable, and stable.
    - This starts the moment you arrive at the courthouse, since you may interact with that judge on your way to the courtroom.
    - Wear a suit to the courthouse and work to portray your best self, similar to [a job interview](jobs-5_interview.md).
  - Work to build credibility, *not* to dominate the room.
  - Avoid filling the silence, which is often awkward but also fine in many contexts.
  - Keep water with you, since you'll be nervous and your mouth will go dry.
- Even in the case of [a forced separation](hardship-separation.md), they will observe facts beyond simply the stories they hear.
  - The stories are the same thing over and over from the accusing party:
    - Alcoholic, drug addict
    - Abusive, harrassing, stalking, violent
    - Terrible provider, financially abusing the children and partner
    - Emotionally negligent, emotionally abusive
- In particular, they focus on specific domains:
  - Which parent is legitimately placing the child's needs first?
  - What does that parent say about the other parent?
  - Even while it's difficult, which parent supports contact between the child and the other parent?
  - Is that parent consistent between what they say and what they've done?
  - Is that parent aware of the child's specific needs (e.g., routines, [schooling](education.md), [health](body-health.md), [emotional wellbeing](mind-feelings.md))?
  - How does that parent behave during court proceedings?
  - How does a parent handle alegations by the other parent?
  - How willing will that parent follow orders?
  - What is the quality of the evidence that parent is presenting?
  - How does that parent approach any resolution?
- There are essentially four factors that judges look for in a court:
  1. Emotional control: can they stay calm under pressure?
     - Lawyers are asking questions designed to evoke a reaction, so don't take the bait and just answer the question.
     - Don't argue with lawyers, since it looks terrible for your case, doesn't mater to them, and they'll look worse for it than you.
  2. Accountability: can they take responsibility for the small things?
     - It's okay to make mistakes, and judges know people aren't perfect.
     - Own your mistakes, since judges can see right through your attempts to hide it (since they deal with it all day).
  3. Clarify: do they answer correctly, or do they ramble?
     - Many people will ramble when they've been asked a simple yes-no question.
     - Many men will destroy their case by talking too much because they're trying to prove that they're right.
     - If you have a question coming up that needs elaboration, ask the attorney if you can offer to explain ahead of time to signal them that they need to revisit the question later.
  4. Consistency: do the answers match the documentation?
     - Never, *ever* [lie](people-lying.md) on the witness stand.
- Take your time when answering questions.
  - The lawyer will ask questions rapid-fire to destabilize you and force you to make a mistake with rapid-fire answers.
  - When receiving the questions face the lawyer, but then face the *judge*, not the lawyer, when answering them.
- At this point, given how many high-conflict mothers use the Silver Bullet strategy, it's now a high-risk proposition for most contexts.
  - In the short-term vistation may be restricted pending review, temporary orders may change, and the litigation becomes more adversarial.
  - However, there are *many* long-term consequences of unsubstantiated, exaggerated, or disproven claims:
    - That person loses credibility with the court.
    - The custody findings will be adverse for them.
    - The court will order evaluations.
    - That person will pay more attorney fees or sanctions.
    - That person will damage coparenting relationships and their child's emotional wellbeing.
- Even small losses can turn into large wins.
  - When a case is decided without prejudice, it often splits things evenly without examining evidence.
  - Your continued compliance will show the judge you're serious about your children.

Be careful what you say to your ex after they've filed for divorce.

- Some things will give them an opportunity for further abuse.
  - Don't tell them your plans once the divorce is finalized.
  - Even if you still love them, don't tell them, since they'll weaponize it against you.
  - Don't indicate that you have any timetable, since they will do everything to keep you legally linked to them.
- Other things will give them an incentive to stalk you and get revenge later.
  - Do *not* tell them you're pleased this is happening.
  - Don't blame them, even if it was their fault.
  - Avoid threatening to take them for everything they have.
- Even after the divorce is finalized, there's a chance they'll weaponize anything you say to the children.

If the divorce is high-conflict, expect it to go longer than you'd ever prefer.

- Court battles are long, messy, and unpleasant for almost everyone except the high-conflict individual.
- The process is mostly the same predictable pattern, no matter how it began or develops:
  1. Flood the system with repeated filings, emergency motions, and procedural tactics to keep the other side overwhelmed.
  2. Collect (or create) information as evidence to portray the other side as unstable, uncooperative, or unsafe (which can often matter more in family court than actual proof).
  3. Interfere with court-ordered contact by blaming logistics or technicalities to create a new standard.
  4. Restrict access to school information, team chats, medical providers, and anything else that prevents the other side from being informed and involved.
  5. Appear cooperative and reasonable to the court while quietly obstructing compliance.
- If you have any direct issues with them, expect them to send confusing and mixed messages about their intent.
- Further, high-conflict individuals will often build clientele through referrals to other high-conflict people, meaning the lawyers are often *very* good at destabilizing their opposition.
  - They'll often train their clients to behave calm and reasonable while trying to magnify the other side as unreasonable or reactionary.
  - This can often include provoking changed behavior, overwhelming with paperwork, amd forcing early decisions.
- In response, you *must* be stable and nonconfrontational.
  - Take your time to write and respond quickly to things in a correct and peaceable way.
  - Take every proactive action you can to move things forward.
  - Openly and quickly communicate any issues ahead of time (e.g., scheduling conflicts).
  - While you don't have to disclose everything, show compliance by communicating information as much as possible with appropriate parties.

If the divorce has become high-conflict, their purpose is simply to harm and destroy you.

- Contrary to what they or others may say, it is *not* for the children's safety or benefit, or for their fair treatment.
  - If it were, they wouldn't be weaponizing the court system against you.
- Unless you're *very* wealthy, the divorce is costing them more money than it's worth:
  - e.g., if a parent requests child support for a 7-year-old, it's 11 years worth of continued support.
    - With a $20,000 lawyer bill, at $500 it will take 40 months to pay it off assuming the parent is steadily paying it (not including [debt](money-2_debt.md) interest), so that means effectively less than 7 years of $500/month after a *lot* of conflict to get it.
- If they cared about the best interests of the child, they wouldn't weaponize the child against the parent who loves them.
  - Barring the possibility of murder, a loving parent would give the child the opportunity to [understand](mind-understanding.md) and [decide](mind-decisions.md) for themselves.
- The motivation *why* they do it varies, but it's generally a sense of entitlement.
  - They believe they have the fully, unfiltered right to everything they ask for, and you are obstructing that and need to be destroyed.
  - They can't tolerate being exposed, so they rewrite everything as a victim narrative to portray you as [evil](morality-evil.md).
- There are often very clear red flags that authorities can detect.
  - They demand the right to 50-50 custody.
  - They're blocking access to their child from authorities or the other parent in some capacity.
  - The children have heard nothing but negative remarks about the other parent.
  - They're obsessed about "their" time, such as preventing the child's extracurricular activities or sleepovers because it takes away from their time.
- Lawyers and judges sometimes see through it, and sometimes don't.
  - The only answer is to document and indicate a pattern with evidence over time, *not* any individual moments of chaos.

Be careful falling into major pitfalls during the divorce.

- Your focus should be on the best interests of the child, *not* on "winning" against your ex.
- Letting [emotions](mind-feelings.md) take over means you may say something you regret.
  - This is often very easy to accomplish for your ex because they will know exactly what [triggers](habits.md) make you emotionally lose control, and will then provoke them in the presence of authority figures (a "dog whistle").
  - Avoid expressing [anger](mind-feelings-anger.md), especially if you hear lies, and focus more on proving the truth.
- Speaking negatively about the other parent is perfectly natural, but it has to be based on facts, and your concerns should solely be for the child's best interests.
- To the degree possible, stay actively involved in the child's school, routines, and healthcare.
- Follow all court orders and parenting agreements to show respect and reliability.
- Communicate clearly, especially [in writing](language-writing.md), but make sure you don't overload the court with irrelevant details.
- Openly address concerns and acknowledge issues, including considering the other parent's perspective and addressing perceived problems.
  - A high-conflict person is very good at looking great for a performance, while you likely aren't as much of a professional at performance, so you need facts to back what you're saying.
  - Avoid making vague statements or accusations, and instead give dates, times and documentation.
    - This should be formatted according to what the judge can easily interpret, so run it through an [LLM](cs-ai.md).
    - You can also create a color-coded calendar that charts the pattern.
    - Make sure to create a different log that indicates the positive qualities of your presence with the child.
- Maintain good behavior inside and outside the courts.
  - In particular, avoid escalating any [conflict](people-conflicts-bad.md) *outside* the courts.
- Stay patient, since your slow and intentional steadiness will build a reputation with the courts.
  - You don't have to be perfect, and simply need to not fold under pressure.

Family court, by its design, is not meant to be punitive.

- It can *create* punitive circumstances, but it doesn't effectively work to punish in its structure.
- Therefore, this creates many implications.
  - If a parent is gatekeeping custody, they will be permitted to keep custody until due process changes it on some type of basis.
  - Filing for contempt will often yield absolutely nothing.
- On the other hand, you yourself won't be held criminally liable in family court, so it's a positive benefit for you as well.

Watch for any false allegations your ex makes against you.

1. Note any repeated allegations and document them carefully.
   - If they keep repeating it, they may be guilty of those actions.
     - If they accuse you of cheating, they're probably cheating.
     - If they're accusing you of being verbally abusive, they are probably doing it.
     - Other strange accusations will often reflect on their behavior if they repeat it (e.g., financial abuse, violence, negligent)
2. Ask for all information during the discovery process through things like depositions, document production requests, interrogatories, and subpoenas.
   - Make it clear that what they're saying is untrue with facts, evidence, and other documentation to prove they're stating falsehoods.
3. Find out as much information possible to support the fact that they are, in fact, doing the behaviors they accuse you of doing.
   - Stay grounded and document everything.
   - Disregard emotion and focus on behaviors, patterns, evidence, and stability.
4. In their testimony in front of a judge, your lawyer should set foundation-building questions that establish absolutes (e.g., always care for the children, perfect parent, etc.), and then use facts to repudiate those circumstances with followup questions.

Carefully consider the [story](stories-why.md) they make against you with their allegations.

- False allegations are typically projected admissions, and come as two forms:
  1. Admissions of their own behaviors, which you need to focus on finding facts that confirm it (interrogatories, request for public documents, subpoenas, etc.).
  2. Admissions of what they're going to attack you with, which prepares you for the [image](people-image.md) they present and how you can respond.
- In whatever capacity possible, build evidence that counters their allegations.
  - The discovery process will find the information they're presenting.
  - Look carefully at your own behaviors to see how true some of those allegations may appear to be.
  - Over time, the truth will show itself, even if your ex has exposed almost everyone to [narcissistic abuse](hardship-abuse-narcissist-stages.md).

The evidence will be quietly built between the court hearings, and then demonstrate itself during the various hearings.

- Courts tend to assess the present state of things and documented patterns, not what has happened previously or what was alleged.
  - For every allegation you make, give 3-5 examples of similar incidents (with dates and times) that show continued behavior.
  - Focus on what kept happening over and over, not on what they're saying about you.
- Often, children can change their opinion in the presence of conflicting information, intensified conflicts at home, or disparaging remarks about the other parent.
- Missing visits or not fulfilling promises can be disastrous to a parent seeking more custody or visitation.

Once you can counter their arguments with facts about them, and your present story repudiates their allegations, you have a better chance of winning your case.

- However, even with guardians ad litem, child advocates, and social services, there's a reasonable chance that courts will overlook the actual best interests of your child.
- Learn to [accept the uncertainty](hardship.md) and do what you can.

Children often learn to silently adapt to the trauma.

- They stop askingquestions because they realize what they say can't change the outcome.
- They learn to behave correctly for each parent's home and in court settings.
- The [uncertainty](mind-understanding-certainty.md) and [emotional pain](hardship-ptsd.md) is so severe that they often learn to hide their true feelings and ignore what they need.

Be careful how you speak about your ex toward your children.

- Stay calm, even when it hurts.
  - If they've been [psychologically manipulated](hardship-abuse-narcissist.md), they'll often state things that are unfair or mean.
  - Avoid reacting emotionally, which requires [learning to find inner peace](mind-awareness.md).
- Don't criticize the other parent, even when their behavior is justified.
  - Children deep-down want the fantasy of a complete reunification of their mother and father, and they will only shift from that during [adolescence](parenting-teenagers.md).
- Learn to [listen](language-speaking-listening.md) more than speak.
  - More than anything, children who have been manipulated need to feel heard more than anything else.
  - You don't have to agree with anything they say, but you can use [empathy](mind-awareness-feelings.md) to echo how they would feel in light of the events they understand.
- Stay neutral and focused on them.
  - If they say something critical about the other parent, acknowledge the feelings without agreeing or disagreeing.
  - If there are truths you want to convey to them, you can always promise to discuss the details with them when they're older, then set that date in your calendar.
- Keep your message simple and consistent.
  - Use short, reassuring statements like "I love you", "I'm here for you", "That won't change", and "I understand" more than anything more complex.
- Don't force closeness or immediate reconciliation.
  - By the time you're attempting to reconnect, they likely have learned how to sever or compartmentalize the other parent just to survive, so they may do it to you as well.
  - Be patient with the process, and focus on protecting your long-term bond with them.

If your ex is a [Cluster B personality](mind-neurodivergence-clusterb.md), they will almost certainly weaponize your relationship with your children.

- They see your children as an extension of themselves, so they will got great lengths to ensure they are fully obedient to everything they demand.
- This will make the situation increasingly dangerous as the children find their own path through the world.
  - Even if you wish to keep the peace, your children are guaranteed to have a difficult and different time than you due to the uniqueness of your divorce compared to you or your ex's experience.
- Get therapy for your children.
  - Many parents miss the symptoms of traumatized children because the children have taken the role of parent with their guardian ("emotional parentification").
  - They need to know they are safe, loved, and worth it, and that will require *years* of therapy to unwind what they're thinking and feeling from what they've been taught to think and suppress.
- Further, they will often work to portray a perfect image to everyone around them.
  - Many times, they will go above and beyond anything reasonable, including [going into debt](money-2_debt.md), for the purpose of harming their ex's reputation.
- The context makes it dangerous because the child has already felt a generally unsafe state with their primary caretaker.
  - That parent feels triggered by the child's need to address emotions, and the child picks up that situation and becomes conditioned to hide their authentic sense of self.
  - Therefore, the child needs a place where they can be their authentic self, but they're unable to express anything without it reflecting as a distortion from that primary caregiver.
  - The child will effectively want to connect and maintain connection with their parent, but can't because their parent is incapable of human connection.
  - In all this, the child won't be able to recognize any of this discrepancy, and will have to figure this out on their own.
  - The child will essentially attempt to defend their abusive parent on every front until they discover the truth, and the only way to combat this context is to set aside your own needs for their benefit.
    - Many parents who are victims of an abusive spouse will focus on weaponizing everything back as revenge on the other parent, and that isn't good for the child at all.

Coparenting during and after divorce is simply not possible with a high-conflict ex, and often harms a child worse than the situation already has become.

- One of the most common methods to create conflict is to have one parent making an opposite demand of the other parent ("double parenting").
  - This exists in [bad parenting in general](parenting-children-bad.md), and severely undermines the child's sense of authority.
- Try to avoid anything that can magnify the issue further.
  - A high-conflict person will use any excuse to fight, so avoid anything they can use as leverage.
  - Avoid leaving a negative impression when transferring by asserting you love them:
    - Instead of saying "Call me anytime, I'm always here", say "Have a great time, I'll see you (future day). I love you".
    - Instead of saying "Be good, don't cause any trouble", say "Be yourself, I love you no matter what"
    - Instead of saying "Text me before you get there/before bed/when you wake up", say "I'll be think of you, see you (future day)"
    - Instead of saying "You can always come home if you want", say "Both homes love you, have fun"
    - Instead of saying "It's okay, you don't have to go if you don't want to", say "I know transitions feel hard, you've got this, I'll be right here when you get back"
    - Instead of saying "I will miss you so much", say "I'm so excited for you, have the best time, I'll see you (future day)"
    - You don't have to [lie](people-lying.md), but you can definitely assert a safe situation for your child.
  - Stay mindful of double standards:
    - Requiring strict compliance to the coparenting plan, but expecting flexibility or exceptions for themselves.
    - Requests additional parenting time but doesn't consistently use the time they already have.
    - Demands prompt communication and responses but delays or ignores communication directed toward them.
    - Insists on strict adherence to schedules but resists reasonable adjustments for vacations, school events, or the child's needs.
    - Criticizes the coparent's decisions while making unilateral decisions without consultation.
    - Accuses their coparent of "putting the children in the middle" while directly involving the children in adult matters or disputes.
    - Raises concerns about the children's well-being in one context but minimizes them when it doesn't support their position.
    - Seeks control over decision-making but avoids accountability for outcomes or inconsistencies.
    - Frames the issues as being in the child's best interests, but setting acctions and communication patterns that create conflict, instability, or stress for the child.
    - Claiming financial concerns over necessary expenses, but not consistently contributing to those expenses or helping meet the child's needs in other ways.
- Instead of coparenting, it's better to pursue "parallel parenting" or "protective parenting", which is the mindset that the other person is either uninvolved or a threat to your children's well-being.
  - This arrangement maintains minimal contact with the other parent and involves a third party doing transfers with the child.
  - Ideally, only use written communication to ensure a paper trail.
  - This may feel like they're gaining control through exclusive access to their child, but it will in fact allow your child to see you and your ex for who they really are (i.e., in a type of vacuum away from the other parent).
- Be [very careful what you say](legal-safety.md) to or about your ex, since *anything* you say can and may be read publicly by a judge later.
- Sharing a calendar will make more information they can use against you later in court.
- Showing up at events together "for the children" will make a host of issues later for your children.
- Be very careful of options provided by courts that aren't aware of the situation.
  - When a court gives an option, many well-meaning parents will take a short-term advantageous solution at the expense of a long-term benefit (e.g., custody only on weekends and pay all child support).
    - Then, the high-conflict parent actions will violate the court-established arrangement, but enough to get away with it (e.g., never showing up on time for custody exchanges).
    - By the time well-intended victims realize the pattern, years sometimes transpire with no remedy, and they never thought to keep records of what happened.
- In particular, be careful about any therapy with both parents involved.
  - Therapy will be another option for your ex to weaponize the system.
    - Often, the therapist will become sympathetic through alleged desire to self-improve, and the therapist will often volunteer information they otherwise wouldn't have given.
    - Further, they will use the therapist's advisement to frame that things are going well.
    - By the time the therapist sees the lies, the parent will then allege the therapist is biased and request another one, which can reset the cycle.
  - Reunification therapy only works when the present parent wants to see the estranged parent reconnect.
    - Unfortunately, it's 18-24 weeks of therapy one hour a week, which is more time for an abusive parent to exploit their custody.
  - Further, any involvement by them with a therapist will teach them how to coach their children over what they should and shouldn't say.
    - Plus, if they subpoena the therapist to testify in court, the child will no longer feel safe to share details with the therapist if it changes the conditions for custody that the child doesn't want.

Be careful about "right of first refusal".

- Most parenting plans give the other parent the first opportunity to babysit the child, but it's often very heavily weaponized.
- In practice, it means a 3-hour date the ex finds out will become evidence to take away custody.
- Set a precise boundary (e.g., over 5 consecutive hours or overnight during your parenting time) to avoid issues.
  - Make sure to require notice with acceptance (e.g., notice by text or email at least 24 hours in advance when possible, and the other parent has 2 hours to accept or 30 minutes for short-notice absences, with non-response deemed a waiver).
  - Make it clear to carve out grandparents because they're family and not a babysitter problem.

In an unfair custody situation, many fathers will receive weekends with the children.

- Healthy fathers will essentially respond by becoming "Disneyland dads".
  - The children will essentially have lots of fun with dad on weekends, and then have to go back with mom on Monday for boring things like school.
- This is adverse for mom, since dad is only fun without discipline.
  - Ironically, this was because of mom's greed, since 50-50 custody would have meant the child would have had a lifestyle that accommodated discipline fully for both parents.

## Responding against the system

If a judge has any reasonable partiality in a case, they must disqualify themselves.

- This [bias](mind-bias.md) doesn't have to be proven beyond reasonable doubt, and simply must be reasonable.

You can definitely take legal action against your lawyer, but it's usually reactive.

- There are some red flags worth observing:
  - They dismiss constitutional arguments immediately.
  - They know the judge personally and try to use their relationship with them instead of the facts.
  - They don't object, push back, or make a record of proof.
  - Their billing is vague or they block how the billing is arranged.
  - They withdraw from the conflict when it becomes high-conflict.
  - They're telling you that you're overthinking, to stop researching, or dismissing your concerns.
  - Months pass and there's no hearing set or motions filed.
  - There are constant continuances and extensions.
  - There's no clear communication policy, and they leave unanswered emails for weeks, the assistant gatekeeps everything, and there's no escalation path.
- Complaining to the state bar association is possible and worth a call, but it may be a waste of time.
  - Sadly, most of the time you have to have an actual injury before the bar or any enforcement agency will do anything.

Making the proceedings public will dramatically complicate things.

- Speaking in general terms is fine, but using any specifics on the internet, especially defamatory remarks (when they're untrue) are enough to create issues in the legal proceedings.

## Rebuilding

After you've grieved for a long while, you can start again:

- Once the pain has worn off, get back in the [dating](relationships-dating.md) world if you don't have [religious reservations](church-family.md) against it.
- Note [any warning signs you keep being attraacted to](relationships-dating-warning.md), especially if they're either just like or the opposite of your ex.
  - Very often, attraction to toxic people means you have unresolved and unconscious [trauma](hardship-ptsd.md) driving your behavior.

If you have to pay child support, it's not a life sentence.

- You'll be required to pay it until the child is 18 (or 19 sometimes if they're in high school).
- As long as you make timely payments, it won't destroy you and your obligation to your ex will be over at that point.
- If there was a very unfair ruling about child support and you're at risk of not paying it:
  - Send a letter to the child support agency to challenge the debt and indicate in writing that it is your belief and information that the child support order and demand payment is a rush to judgment into a default judgment and both are a violation of your constitutionally protected rights, and make sure to sign it as your claim under penalty of perjury.

You're guaranteed to have a "rebound" relationship if you get into one the first few years afterward, so don't take *anything* seriously or steadily for at least 2-3 years.

- As a general rule, spend about one year of recovery for every 4 years of marriage to grieve.
  - We must give ourselves permission to [grieve](mind-feelings-sadness.md) or we will escape into an [addiction](addiction.md).
- The experience can be addictive in finding a new partner, so avoid finding someone new until you've gotten the rest of your new lifestyle figured out.
- It doesn't mean it'll take 10 years to recover from a 40-year divorce, but recovery will probably take longer than you may expect.
  - give at least 1 month for every year of connection with that person, though it can be shorter if you face the emotional work head-on without escapism.
- There are some key indicators to know you're moving forward emotionally.
  - You're not consumed by thoughts of your ex.
    - You accept their [successes](success-1_why.md).
    - You're not comparing yourself with them
    - You become upset about things *not* related to the ex.
  - You don't expect the ex to behave a certain way.
    - This means no longer expecting any sense of "they owe me" for anything.
    - This also means [setting healthy boundaries](people-boundaries.md) with the ex.
  - You do things that make you [happy](mind-feelings-happiness.md) like hobbies and personal activities.
  - you're content with yourself and have found closure as a single person again.
  - you've found peace with the [unknown](unknown.md) and your relationship with God improves.
    - This also means you're not trying to figure out the future anymore (since you won't find any more [meaning](meaning.md) from figuring it out).

## How to work through the trauma of divorcing parents

Even if you're in your 40s, you'll feel like your world is falling apart:

- This won't be any simpler with all the new people involved in the situation:
  - Lawyers
  - Well-meaning relatives
  - Possible boyfriend/girlfriend of a parent
  - Social workers
  - Extended family of that parent's new partner

It's natural to feel very unsafe, as well as the urge to fix your parents' issues:

- Your parents were the first representation of [love](people-love.md) and [order](mind-understanding-certainty.md) in your life, and you must discover another source for your comfort and safety.
- Their problems run deeper than you could ever know (likely before you were born), and you will *not* be able to fix the situation.

Hang on and don't think much about what will happen:

- The younger you are, the more your life will flip upside-down.
- The dust will settle in the end, and that's when you can make sense of what happened.
- 2/3 of children in divorces run across major issues in their life, even after 5-10 years, so take the issues as they come.

Don't worry too much about the new unspoken rules:

- No matter what happens, you'll technically be shifting into a new [culture](people-culture.md), so don't expect to adapt to the new lifestyle right away.
- Focus on surviving and rebuilding a life of your own, *away* from either of your parents.
- In time, you'll learn to [find happiness](mind-feelings-happiness-focus.md) in spite of the chaos.
