
# How to correctly write nonfiction

These are the necessary aspects that work solely for writing effective nonfiction.

## The messaging

All great messages contain a WHAT and a WHY:

- WHAT communicates the information and events you want to share.
  - Event details, date, time, location
  - Educational information, data, facts
- WHY communicates the benefits from knowing the information or the cause for the WHAT.
  - It gives the [reason](purpose.md) you're writing at all.
  - It answers the "call to action" the reader must take.
- The rest spins off from these two:
  - WHO is the character (or reader) that does WHAT.
  - WHERE is the setting for WHAT.
  - WHEN is the chronology of WHAT.
  - HOW is the detailed set of processes to explain the WHAT and WHY.

## Specific forms of writing

When using examples and anecdotes, carefully consider your quantity:

- 1 demonstrates raw power.
- 2 is useful to compare or contrast.
- 3 gives a sense of completeness, fullness, or wholeness.
- 4 or more can list, inventory, compile, and expand.
- In reality, 3 gives a stronger sense of completeness than 4.

Focus on the positive form of the idea:

- Say "do" instead of "don't".
- What something "isn't" is less informative than what something "is".

Always state who is acting.

- Using the passive voice will both confuse the reader and imply you don't know who acted.

## Specific words

Transition correctly with "signal words":

- Descriptive language indicates an expansion of an idea (for example, in particular, to illustrate, most important, in addition).
- Sequence language gives an indication of multiple elements (when, first, next, before, third, following).
- Comparison gives a demarcation between two things (like, likewise, unlike, although, in contrast).
- Cause-and-effect indicates a sequence (because of, for this reason, may be caused by, since, therefore).

Replace uncommon words whenever possible.

- You're writing *for* them, not against them, so the words should make it as easy as possible for the reader to understand.

Delete empty words that don't add any meaning.

Replace adjectives with verbs.

Use examples that are interesting and descriptive instead of simply more abstractions.
